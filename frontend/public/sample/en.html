A corner folded down

Eselsohr · a sample book

Before bookmarks were cheap, readers folded down the corner of a page to find their place again. In German that fold is an Eselsohr, a donkey’s ear, because the bent paper sticks up like one.

This page is a small book, made the way yours will be. Eselsohr fetched an article, kept the words and the pictures, left the menus, cookie banners and ads behind, and bound the rest into an EPUB.

Why read it on an e-reader

Long articles are easier on a screen that looks like paper. There are no notifications, no video that starts by itself, and no sidebar asking you to subscribe. The text waits where you left it, and the battery lasts for weeks.

Most of us save more than we read. Tabs pile up, reading lists grow, and the good piece from Tuesday is gone by Friday. Sending it to the device you already read books on puts it next to your books, which is where long reading tends to happen.

What happens to a link

  1. Eselsohr loads the page and finds the article in it.
  2. Title, author, date and language come from the page itself.
  3. Images are converted to formats every Kindle shows, and made smaller so the book stays light.
  4. The book goes to your Kindle by email, or you download it for any other reader.

A look before it goes anywhere

Every book shows up on a little reader like this one before it is sent, so a missing paragraph or a broken image doesn’t end up in your library. If something looks wrong, you can tell the admin which page needs fixing.
